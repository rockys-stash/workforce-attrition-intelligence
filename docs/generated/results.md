## Recommendations

1. **Review overtime load** (Workload). 31% of employees who work overtime left, against 10% of those who do not. Evidence: Within the same job level the odds of having left are 4.1 times as high with overtime (Mantel–Haenszel, 95% CI 3.0–5.5); Adjusted for the other model variables, odds ratio 6.75 (95% CI 4.66–9.77); Overtime is most common among Research Scientist (33%), Sales Representative (29%), Sales Executive (29%). Action: Look at how overtime is distributed and whether it is planned; if it is reduced somewhere, compare attrition there with comparable teams before drawing conclusions. Basis: Association in a cross-section of a fictional workforce; no change has been tested here.
2. **Pay attention to the first two years** (Tenure). 35% of employees in their first two years (0–1 yrs) left, against 16% overall. Evidence: 75 of the 237 leavers (32%) had under two years at the company; Years at the company is entangled with age and job level; the tenure page shows both. Action: Check in with new joiners at fixed points in their first two years and keep exit-interview records, so later data can separate tenure from age and level. Basis: Association in a cross-section of a fictional workforce; no change has been tested here.
3. **Review pay position at entry level** (Compensation). Level 1 has the highest attrition (26%), and employees paid more than 10% below their level's median left more often (20%) than those near it (15%). Evidence: Level 1 holds 143 of 237 leavers; Adjusted for job level, tenure and the other model variables, the odds ratio per 1,000 of monthly income is 1.01 (95% CI 0.86–1.18); pay and level move together (variance inflation 11), so the two are hard to separate. Action: Compare entry-level pay with the market and within roles; treat pay changes as something to evaluate, not as a known lever. Basis: Association in a cross-section of a fictional workforce; no change has been tested here.
4. **Use low satisfaction scores as an early signal** (Engagement). Employees reporting low job satisfaction left more often (23%) than those reporting very high satisfaction (11%). Evidence: Low environment satisfaction: 25% left; Satisfaction was recorded once, with no date, so it may have been measured after the decision to leave. Action: Follow up low survey scores with a conversation; re-survey regularly so satisfaction can be measured before anyone leaves. Basis: Association in a cross-section of a fictional workforce; no change has been tested here.
5. **Check who has no stock options** (Compensation). 24% of employees without stock options left, against 10% of those with any. Evidence: 631 employees have none; 100% of single employees have no stock options, and 26% of single employees left, so stock options and marital status cannot be separated in these data. Action: Review eligibility rules for stock options before reading anything into this pattern. Basis: Association in a cross-section of a fictional workforce; no change has been tested here.
6. **Look closely at the Sales Representative role** (Departments). 40% of Sales Representatives left (33 of 83), the highest rate of any role with at least 30 employees. Evidence: Next: Laboratory Technician 24%, Human Resources 23%; Overall rate 16%. Action: Review the role's workload, pay position and tenure profile on the Departments page and talk to the team. Basis: Association in a cross-section of a fictional workforce; no change has been tested here.
